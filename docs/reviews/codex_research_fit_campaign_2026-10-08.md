## Executive summary

- **Use stream-aligned coordinates for reporting, but let the track curve.** A great-circle frame is a useful coordinate system and zero-point, not a physical assumption that the stream follows one great circle. For these data, fit a robust, curved, two-dimensional path and estimate proper motion, distance, radial velocity, and width as functions of position along it.
- **Treat the published membership lists as selected data.** STREAMFINDER scores stars against orbit templates, so fitting the same list’s mean track can inherit template bias. Use the lists for an initial measurement, then compare against an independently selected Gaia sample or quantify the bias with injection and recovery through STREAMFINDER.
- **Start the model search with sprays, then validate against the existing tracer runs.** Sprays can integrate debris in a rotating, time-dependent bar potential and are suitable for broad parameter exploration. Their escape prescription is approximate; progenitor spin and the substantial cluster mass make a targeted higher-fidelity comparison important. The newer Chen et al. spray paper reports about 10% morphology and velocity accuracy against its N-body tests, not a guarantee for this particular orbit.
- **Fit the bar and host before testing cluster dark matter.** First find the acceptable bar and host region while marginalizing over ω Cen’s present-day phase space. Then compare the no-DM, moderate-DM, and 5×-DM-inside-200-pc cases by refitting nuisance parameters under each hypothesis and testing recovery on mock data.
- **The stream data supplied here favor a bar, but do not yet identify a unique bar.** The stated proper-motion gradient agreement at \(\Omega_b=33\) and partial sky-track bend are encouraging. A single short, selected stream segment is unlikely to separate bar pattern speed, bar strength, host structure, and progenitor phase space without external priors and independent track validation.

## Detailed findings

### A. Measuring the observed track

**Standard approaches.** A stream-aligned great-circle rotation, such as the GD-1 \((\phi_1,\phi_2)\) frame, is a convenient way to define along- and cross-stream coordinates. It works well for organizing data and fitting a nearly planar stream, but it does not require the stream to be planar. The GD-1 work established this style of coordinate system and showed how a stream’s phase-space map can be fit; the Orphan–Chenab analysis later demonstrates why a long stream’s pole can drift substantially. [Koposov, Rix & Hogg 2010, *ApJ*; arXiv:0907.1085, DOI:10.1088/0004-637X/712/1/260](https://arxiv.org/abs/0907.1085) [Koposov et al. 2019, *MNRAS*; arXiv:1812.08192, DOI:10.1093/mnras/stz457](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.08192)

For a local, clean stream segment, a low-order polynomial or spline in \(\phi_2(\phi_1)\) is a practical track model. Erkal et al.’s Pal 5 analysis jointly modeled a spline track, width, density, and background. The S⁵ Orphan–Chenab analysis reconstructed sky track, proper motion, radial velocity, distance, and density with spline models. Ferguson et al.’s Jet analysis likewise used natural splines for track, width, and intensity. These methods provide interpretable tracks and uncertainties, but standard one-dimensional splines assume the stream is a function of the chosen along-stream coordinate. [Erkal, Koposov & Belokurov 2017, *MNRAS*; arXiv:1609.01282, DOI:10.1093/mnras/stw2357](https://arxiv.org/abs/1609.01282) [Koposov et al. 2023, *MNRAS*; arXiv:2211.04495, DOI:10.1093/mnras/stad551](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.04495) [Ferguson et al. 2021, *ApJ*; arXiv:2104.11755, DOI:10.3847/1538-4357/ac0e2a](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.11755)

For a strongly curved path, a method that reconstructs the path without forcing \(\phi_2\) to be a single-valued function of \(\phi_1\) is safer. Starkman et al.’s TrackStream uses self-organizing maps and Kalman filtering; the authors report that it handles sparse data and phase-wrapped paths while propagating path uncertainty. A full probabilistic alternative is the mixture-density approach of Starkman et al., which jointly estimates track, width, density, kinematics, membership, and background. [Starkman et al. 2023, *MNRAS*; arXiv:2212.00949, DOI:10.1093/mnras/stad100](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.00949) [Starkman et al. 2023, *ApJ*; arXiv:2311.16960, DOI:10.3847/1538-4357/ad00f0](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.16960)

**What STREAMFINDER contributes and what it does not.** The DR3 atlas paper constructs a separate great-circle frame for each stream, with coordinates oriented along the stream velocity; for known globular-cluster progenitors it anchors the frame on the cluster. This is a useful convention for comparing streams and models. The same paper says that sky-position errors are negligible for its great-circle fitting, an assumption that is usually safe for Gaia positions but says nothing about uncertain membership or selection. [Ibata et al. 2024, “Charting the Galactic Acceleration Field II,” peer-reviewed published version; arXiv:2311.17202](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.17202) [Cambridge repository record](https://www.repository.cam.ac.uk/items/5b6c8222-ddc1-4583-9bc1-257155bb608d)

STREAMFINDER evaluates stars against trial-orbit templates and a stream “hypertube,” using astrometric, kinematic, and population information. Consequently, a member list selected this way is not a random sample of the stream’s true phase-space distribution. The selection can favor stars that lie near the templates used in the search. [Malhan & Ibata 2018, “STREAMFINDER—I,” *MNRAS*; DOI:10.1093/mnras/sty1036](https://academic.oup.com/mnras/article/477/3/4063/4969694) The DR3 atlas paper’s reported streams and stellar masses also depend on the extent of the identified stream, its assumed stellar population, and a completeness assumption; it explicitly describes some mass estimates as rough. [Ibata et al. 2024; arXiv:2311.17202](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.17202)

**Practical recommendation for streams 54 and 55.**

1. Set a fixed spherical coordinate frame for each candidate, anchored on ω Cen and with the initial along-stream axis chosen from the observed arm. Keep that frame fixed for both data and all models. Use it to label \(\phi_1,\phi_2\), not to impose a great-circle shape.
2. Measure a curved sky path with a robust spline or TrackStream-like path estimator. Fit robust location and intrinsic scatter in \(\phi_2\), both proper-motion components, distance, and radial velocity as functions of path length or a locally monotonic \(\phi_1\). Fit the **distance modulus offset track** as well as any available absolute-distance estimates, with a separate zero-point nuisance term.
3. Use bins or adaptive windows large enough to yield stable estimates. Within each, use a contamination-resistant location estimate or a stream/background mixture model, and carry through Gaia’s per-star astrometric covariance. Estimate uncertainty by resampling spatial blocks or using a fitted covariance model; ordinary bootstrap resampling of individual stars would understate errors if nearby selected members have correlated selection.
4. Report the fitted path, width, and kinematic tracks with a covariance matrix or posterior samples. Keep radial-velocity points at their actual locations rather than smoothing them into falsely precise bins. The user-supplied member counts and velocity counts are analysis inputs here; they are not independently verified by the cited papers.
5. **Do not use member counts or density as a dynamical likelihood initially.** Unknown STREAMFINDER completeness, crowding, magnitude limits, and foreground contamination make density harder to interpret than the centroid track. A later density analysis requires a selection model.

To assess template bias, repeat the track measurement using a Gaia parent sample selected independently of STREAMFINDER—for example, a broad sky/colour/magnitude region followed by a cross-validated mixture or chemical/kinematic selection. If this cannot be assembled immediately, treat the STREAMFINDER track as a potentially biased summary: add a systematic track-error floor and exclude the most template-sensitive portion from the primary fit. The strongest check is injection and recovery: pass synthetic stream stars through the original selection and measure how much the recovered track shifts. Gaia subset selection can itself have strong spatial, magnitude, and colour dependence; this is documented for Gaia DR3 subsets generally. [Castro-Ginard et al. 2023, “Estimating the selection function of Gaia DR3 sub-samples”; arXiv:2303.17738](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.17738)

**Keep streams 54 and 55 separate at first.** The DR3 atlas labels #54 Fimbulthul and #55 Fimbulthul-S; the atlas highlights the nearby trailing ω Cen arm. Separately, recent spectroscopy reports that stream #55 is in the same action–energy region as Fimbulthul. That is evidence for a relationship, not proof that the two observed sequences are one continuous arm or were selected without overlap. Fit each separately, check for duplicate Gaia sources, and then test a joint model with a shared host and progenitor but separate membership/selection components. [Ibata et al. 2024; arXiv:2311.17202](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.17202) [“Disruption of a giant,” *MNRAS* (2026)](https://academic.oup.com/mnras/article/550/2/stag1147/8709286)

### B. Generating model tracks

**Fast options.**

- **Orbit-only models** are useful for initial exploration, but an orbit is not a stream. Stream–orbit offsets matter, especially for a massive, disrupting progenitor and for the knee.
- **Particle spray / Lagrange-point stripping** releases tracer particles near the progenitor’s tidal boundary and integrates them in the host potential. Fardal et al. calibrated an ejection prescription against N-body models; Gibbons et al. developed a related particle-release method. These methods are fast and naturally include subsequent perturbations to already released particles. Their weaknesses are approximate escape physics, simplified mass loss, and errors near the progenitor or for complicated stripping histories. [Fardal, Huang & Weinberg 2015, *MNRAS*; arXiv:1410.1861, DOI:10.1093/mnras/stv1198](https://arxiv.org/abs/1410.1861) [Gibbons, Belokurov & Evans 2014, *MNRAS*; arXiv:1406.2243, DOI:10.1093/mnras/stu692](https://arxiv.org/abs/1406.2243)
- **Improved spray.** Chen et al. (2024 preprint) calibrate release distributions against cluster N-body runs and report roughly 10% accuracy in stream morphology and velocities in their tests. Their implementation is reported for AGAMA, gala, galax, and galpy. This is a promising first choice, but its published accuracy is not a validated error budget for ω Cen on this orbit, with this bar and this internal rotation. [Chen et al. 2024, preprint; arXiv:2408.01496](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.01496)
- **Restricted N-body / tracer integrations** are the natural middle ground when the cluster potential is prescribed: integrate a cluster model or sampled tracers in the external potential, with whatever cluster–tracer force treatment the existing runs use. The supplied local timing—3.4 minutes for axisymmetric and 6.5 minutes for barred models on eight threads—is especially valuable: it provides a measured, higher-fidelity validation tier.
- **Live direct N-body** is the reference for internal cluster dynamics, mass loss, and rotation, but is expensive for broad bar-parameter fitting. Reserve it for a small number of calibration models if the fixed-potential tracer treatment cannot capture the relevant escape physics.

**Time-dependent bar and progenitor spin.** A spray is mathematically compatible with a time-dependent host: integrate the progenitor orbit in the rotating potential, release particles at their modeled escape times, then integrate each released particle in that same time-dependent potential. gala documents mock-stream generation with rotating/non-static reference frames; AGAMA’s Hunter et al. example constructs an explicitly rotating bar potential with a specified present-day phase and pattern speed. These establish implementation feasibility, not accuracy of a particular spray prescription under bar-driven chaotic or strongly varying tides. [gala mock-stream documentation](https://gala.adrian.pw/en/stable/dynamics/mockstreams.html) [AGAMA Hunter+2024 example](https://github.com/GalacticDynamics-Oxford/Agama/blob/master/py/example_mw_potential_hunter24.py)

Internal rotation deserves explicit attention here. The original Fimbulthul N-body interpretation used an ω Cen model with internal rotation; a recent Pal 5 discussion also summarizes that the ω Cen–Fimbulthul models require some level of progenitor rotation. A standard isotropic spray does not automatically encode spin-dependent escape. Treat progenitor spin direction and amplitude as an uncertainty in calibration runs, or use a spin-aware release model validated against rotating-cluster N-body simulations. Do not let an uncalibrated spray spin prescription decide the dark-matter result. [Ibata et al. 2019, *Nature Astronomy*; arXiv:1902.09544, DOI:10.1038/s41550-019-0751-x](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.09544) [“Forward and back: kinematics of the Palomar 5 tidal tails,” *MNRAS* (2022)](https://doi.org/10.1093/mnras/stac381)

**Cost.** There is no defensible universal “seconds per model” from the papers: it depends on particle count, integrator, hardware, bar representation, and code. For campaign planning, benchmark a small grid of spray models first, then use the measured median and 90th-percentile runtime. The supplied tracer timings imply that 100 such runs would cost about 5–11 hours on the same eight-thread setup, before analysis overhead; that makes tracer runs suitable for validation and targeted refinement, not a large sampler. A spray search can be budgeted by wall-clock after the benchmark rather than assumed free.

**Build model summaries identically to data.** Transform model particles to the same heliocentric frame and stream coordinates; apply identical magnitude/colour cuts, measurement errors, distance-modulus treatment, velocity availability, and bin/window locations. For each data bin, measure model medians/means and intrinsic scatter with the same robust estimator used on the observations. Convolve model observables with observational covariance before evaluating the fit. Apply either a Gaia completeness model and contamination model or an approximate STREAMFINDER selection function; otherwise omit density terms. If STREAMFINDER injection/recovery becomes available, apply the same recovery process to mocks and data.

### C. Fitting strategy and parameter identifiability

**Objective.** A practical likelihood is a multivariate Gaussian (or Student-t / mixture) likelihood for the track observables in each bin:

\[
\log L = -\frac12 \sum_j
(\mathbf y_j-\mathbf m_j)^\mathsf{T}
\left(\mathbf C_{\mathrm{obs},j}+\mathbf C_{\mathrm{model},j}+\mathbf C_{\mathrm{sys},j}\right)^{-1}
(\mathbf y_j-\mathbf m_j)
-\frac12\sum_j \log |\mathbf C_j|.
\]

Here \(\mathbf y_j\) contains sky position, proper motion, and available distance and radial velocity; \(\mathbf m_j\) is the matched model summary. Include intrinsic model scatter, measurement covariance, and a systematic floor for selection and track-estimation uncertainty. Avoid treating every selected star as an independent likelihood contribution when the actual objective is a smooth track. Erkal et al.’s Orphan analysis adds observational and simulated-stream variances in a per-star likelihood; the S⁵ Orphan–Chenab work fits a six-dimensional track with spline summaries and stripping models. [Erkal et al. 2019, *MNRAS*; arXiv:1812.08192, DOI:10.1093/mnras/stz1371](https://arxiv.org/abs/1812.08192) [Koposov et al. 2023; arXiv:2211.04495](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.04495)

Fit the knee as part of the curved track, not as an isolated angle measurement. A good secondary diagnostic is its curvature or a predeclared local displacement from a smooth reference track, evaluated over a fixed \(\phi_1\) interval. This makes it harder for flexible splines to erase the feature or for model binning to move it.

**Parameters and priors.**

- **Bar:** pattern speed \(\Omega_b\), present-day angle, amplitude or mass normalization, and at most one bar-size/shape parameter. Start with the supplied Hunter et al. potential family. Use external information as a prior or sensitivity range, not as an absolute truth: published pattern-speed estimates differ substantially by method. For example, Portail et al.’s made-to-measure model reports \(39.0\pm3.5\ {\rm km\,s^{-1}\,kpc^{-1}}\), while Clarke & Gerhard report \(33.29\pm1.81\) from a different bulge proper-motion comparison. [Portail et al. 2017, *MNRAS*; arXiv:1608.07954, DOI:10.1093/mnras/stw2819](https://arxiv.org/abs/1608.07954) [Clarke & Gerhard 2022, *MNRAS*; arXiv:2107.10875, DOI:10.1093/mnras/stab3451](https://arxiv.org/abs/2107.10875)
- **Host:** initially fix or tightly regularize the disk and halo to the Hunter model; then vary a small number of parameters such as halo normalization/flattening or disk mass/scale. The DR3 atlas supplies one useful axisymmetric-stream prior, but it is not a barred-stream fit and should not be imported as a precise constraint on this orbit. [Ibata et al. 2024; arXiv:2311.17202](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.17202)
- **Progenitor:** use Gaussian priors for ω Cen’s current six-dimensional phase-space coordinates based on the adopted catalogue and Gaia astrometry, including their covariances. Keep these as nuisance parameters: even a modest shift can alter where the released debris falls on the sky.
- **Release physics:** stream age / release-time range, progenitor mass and scale, and spin parameters (or a calibrated fixed spin prescription). The supplied estimate that material at \(b>30^\circ\) was released 300–600 Myr ago is a prior from the stated local analysis, not an independently verified literature constraint.

Pattern speed and present-day angle both influence bar phase at past stripping times; bar strength trades off against the host’s non-axisymmetric force and progenitor phase space. Halo flattening, disk mass, and bar amplitude can all shift the track. Distance shifts and solar-reflex corrections affect proper-motion interpretation; sparse radial velocities limit how much they can break degeneracies. The knee plus the observed proper-motion gradient can constrain different aspects of the time-dependent perturbation, but only if the same track is measured without template imprint.

**Optimizer and sampling.** Use a coarse grid or Latin-hypercube / CMA-ES search to find viable basins; refine with a derivative-free local optimizer if sprays introduce Monte Carlo noise. Then sample the posterior with an ensemble MCMC or nested sampler using fixed random seeds/common random numbers where possible, to reduce simulation noise in parameter differences. An emulator is worthwhile only after the parameter-to-summary map is smooth enough and its prediction error is checked on withheld models. At seconds-to-minutes per model, fitting four bar parameters plus many host and progenitor parameters with brute-force MCMC is a poor starting point.

**The specific detectability test:** compare three discrete hypotheses—no DM, moderate DM, and 5× DM inside 200 pc—using the same data likelihood, priors, and selection treatment. Marginalize over bar, host, ω Cen’s present phase space, and release/spin parameters separately under each hypothesis. Report posterior model probabilities or Bayes factors, plus a posterior-predictive separation in the joint track/PM/distance/velocity summaries.

Call the DM effect **measurable** only if (a) the preferred DM hypothesis remains favored after nuisance refitting and after plausible track-selection systematics are included, and (b) blinded mock recovery distinguishes that hypothesis with calibrated false-positive rate and useful power. A practical initial bar is a Bayes factor above 100 (\(\Delta\ln Z>4.6\)) **and** successful recovery in mocks; this is a campaign decision threshold, not a universal scientific standard. If the streams’ widths or velocities are what carry the separation, include them only after confirming their measurement and selection models. A difference between best-fit tracks alone is not evidence of detection if the bar or progenitor nuisance parameters can absorb it.

## Disagreements, uncertainties, and open questions

- **Stream #55’s relationship to Fimbulthul is not settled by shared action–energy location.** The label “Fimbulthul-S” and spectroscopy support analyzing it as a plausible ω Cen-associated structure, but neither source establishes that its sample is an unbiased continuation of #54. Keep the fits separate until the data support a joint model.
- **Published bar pattern speeds disagree.** The estimates cited above arise from different dynamical tracers and model assumptions. The stated local fit at \(\Omega_b=33\) is consistent with one published estimate and distinct from others; it should be interpreted as a stream-specific fit conditional on the host, bar model, and membership track.
- **Spray accuracy is not yet demonstrated for this exact problem.** Chen et al.’s roughly 10% result comes from its simulation suite. The ω Cen orbit is massive, rotating, and passes through a barred, time-dependent field; the tracer cross-check is essential.
- **Selection uncertainty can dominate formal track errors.** The STREAMFINDER membership list is useful and information-rich, but its orbit-template selection makes the track measurement partly endogenous. Without independent Gaia selection or injection/recovery, uncertainty bars from the selected members alone are too optimistic.
- **A single segment may not constrain bar slowdown.** With the supplied 300–600 Myr release-time range and current stream coverage, fit a constant pattern speed first. Slowdown adds a time-history degree of freedom that is likely degenerate with bar strength, phase, and progenitor initial conditions; add it only if a constant-speed posterior fails structured residual checks and mocks show recoverability.
- **“5× DM inside 200 pc” needs an explicit density profile and time evolution.** The enclosed dark mass alone does not define the tidal force at the cluster’s limiting radius or its past mass loss. Before interpreting the three hypotheses, specify how DM is spatially distributed, whether it is bound to ω Cen, and how that component evolves under stripping.
- **No published source fixes the campaign’s runtime.** The supplied 3.4/6.5-minute tracer timing is a local measured result. Spray runtime should be benchmarked in the actual implementation before assigning a firm posterior-sampling budget.
