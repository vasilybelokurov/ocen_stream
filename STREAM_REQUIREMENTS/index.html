    What the omega Cen stream (stream 54) requires to be fitted well¶

    Status: 2026-10-22. Summary figure: plots/stream_requirements.png (bin/streams/requirements_figure.py).

    Summary¶

    • A good fit needs a non-axisymmetric force at R = 4-7 kpc about 2.5-3.5 times that of the published Milky Way bar, locked to the bar (co-rotating, in phase). With the bar's strength and size free, every good fit inflates the Hunter+2024 / Sormani+2022 bar to amplitude A ~ 1.4 and size S ~ 1.25-1.33. Its quadrupole is then ~2.6-3.2 times the published one; inside 3 kpc the increase is only 1.2-1.4 times (panel a).
    • With the bar fixed at its published strength, size and angle (27 deg), the best fit is 2,627 worse in ln L than the best inflated-bar fit, and even that needs an ultrafast bar (Omega_b = 44.8 km/s/kpc; panels b, c). The failure is mainly mu_alpha* in the middle of the stream, where the model is 1-3 mas/yr too negative at phi1 7-19 deg: the PM turn starts too early.
    • The stream measures a torque history, not a static force. Along omega Cen's orbit the extra force averages to zero (<= 0.003 |F_axi| over 300-700 Myr) but its rms is about 2x the published bar's. omega Cen's L_z swings more widely (panel d), and the 300-700-Myr-old debris records that history. No axisymmetric change (disc, halo flattening, v_c, solar frame, distance, PMs) can replace it.
    • Within the inflated-bar model the stream prefers a low bar angle (12.5-17.5 deg; 25 deg is 1,071 worse, 27.5 deg 1,885 worse). The preference survives every test below. It is conditional on the inflated bar.
    • The demanded bar conflicts with local constraints: its m=2 strength at the solar radius is 0.062 |F_axi|, against 0.01-0.02 in Hercules/OLR models. Equivalently, the long bar would need to be ~2x as massive as Sormani+2022's (total bar ~2.4e10 vs 1.83e10 Msun) and ~1.3x longer, reaching corotation (~6.4 kpc, against 5.0 +- 0.2 kpc measured).

    Model and data¶

    • Members: STREAMFINDER (Ibata+2024) stream 54 within the stream's footprint; Gaia DR3 PMs with covariances; 29 v_los.
    • Model: Fardal-type particle spray from omega Cen in the Hunter+2024 barred Milky Way. The bar is A x [baryon_full(x/S) - baryon_axi(x/S)] rotating at Omega_b; the axisymmetric part uses an Einasto halo with flattening q, normalised to v_c(R0).
    • Frame and priors: gravity21 solar frame (R0 8.275 kpc, Sgr A* PM, z_sun 20.8 pc). omega Cen's distance is held in 5.33-5.53 kpc, with priors on the distance, systemic PMs and v_c (233 +- 7 km/s).
    • Score: conditional likelihood p(Delta phi2, PMs, v_los | phi1) with fixed chi bins 0-200, jackknife-corrected; debris younger than 700 Myr.
    • Parameters (9): bar angle, Omega_b, A, S, d, mu_alpha*, mu_delta, v_c, q.
    • Statistics: the score is overconfident by T ~ 2 (calibrated on 20 mock catalogues, bin/streams/tempering_calibration.py). The star bootstrap gives the right sampling errors; the block bootstrap overstates them 2-6x. Statistical sd of angle-to-angle differences is ~30-80 in ln L.

    What the stream does not require (tested and ruled out as substitutes)¶

    • An axisymmetric change: these cannot mimic a torque history. Halo flattening and disc mass were tested on 10-10 and all made the fit worse; disc_scale was not refitted in the current setup.
    • Spiral arms at the published amplitude.
    • A slowing bar (tested on 10-10 in the old frame: worse at every pattern speed).
    • A release-history change (pericentric bursts).
    • A selection effect: the Gaia search finds no off-track debris.

    Remaining misfits, common to every model tried¶

    • Delta phi2 offsets: -1.2 to -2 deg at phi1 3-15 and +1.2 to +2.3 deg at 25-27 deg.
    • Knee mu_delta 0.5-1 mas/yr too shallow (phi1 > 19); knee debris 7-10% too close against the CMD distance track.
    • Fit pulled to range edges: distance at the band top (5.52-5.53 kpc), v_c 223-226 km/s.
    • Along-stream density: the members' ~10x gap at phi1 6-13 is not reproduced (the score does not use the density).

    Interpretation¶

    The stream constrains a bar-locked, non-axisymmetric force history along omega Cen's retrograde orbit (pericentre 1.5-2 kpc, apocentre 6.4-7.2 kpc) more firmly than it constrains the bar angle. Within the Hunter bar family this force comes out ~2x the published bar's outer force; with it, the angle is low (~12-18 deg). The published bar cannot fit the stream at any pattern speed in 20-50. The most physical candidate not yet tested is a co-rotating "dark bar", the halo's response to the stellar bar (Petersen+2016), which extends beyond the stellar bar. Whether it can supply the force without violating the solar-neighbourhood constraints is open.

    Statistical vs systematic uncertainty¶

    With T ~ 2 the formal (statistical) errors are tiny: angle ~0.3-1 deg, Omega_b ~0.1, A ~0.02. The posterior's 68% region spans Delta lnL ~ 10, below the simulation noise (sd 14-35 at 256k). Model changes move the best fit 50-500 times further, so the uncertainty is systematic and is represented by the spread between model variants. A Laplace covariance for the final model is planned once the model is settled (docs/reviews/fable_posterior_revisited_20261022.md).

    Next steps¶

    1. A co-rotating dark-bar (halo-wake) component as the physical source of the extra force.
    2. A Hercules / solar-neighbourhood check of the demanded bar (U-V distribution with test particles, against Gaia).
    3. With the final model: a Laplace covariance, and per-segment residuals.
