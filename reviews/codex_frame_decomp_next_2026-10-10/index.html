    Assessment¶

    “Orbit, not projection” is too broad for the total likelihood, but supported for the reported knee track change. The V⊙,y-only control changes the cluster’s Galactocentric initial velocity, while leaving the solar position parameters fixed; the packet reports a +620 dφ₂ score change in the knee. Since the sky coordinates are calculated from particle positions, that change must come through the changed initial condition and resulting particle positions, not a direct velocity projection effect. The code converts the cluster observables to an initial condition using frame, then projects particles using proj_frame (grid4_common.py:46–51; frames.py:31–39, 48–56). The reported control and score are in the packet (packet:2021–2029).

    That does not establish that the whole I/I likelihood gain is an orbit effect. The consistent I/I run gains 841 over B/B, but the crossed runs score 345 and 3,011 below B/B; switching IC frame at Ibata projection changes the score by 3,852, while switching it at Baumgardt projection changes it by −345 (packet:2017–2028). Those are large interactions. Also, the mixed-frame runs are diagnostic controls, not self-consistent observer frames: they start the cluster in one frame and convert the debris using another. So the evidence supports an IC-driven knee-track improvement plus a substantial projection/IC interaction, not a clean decomposition of the total score.

    One premise needs qualification: dφ₂ and φ₁ are computed from sky positions, but those positions are outcomes of the orbit integration. A V⊙ change can therefore alter dφ₂ through the initial velocity and subsequent trajectory even though it does not directly change the sky-coordinate transformation for fixed positions. The fixed chord frame is applied to the resulting model \(l,b\) (grid4_common.py:17–18, 54–56; path.py:67–77).

    Physical causes and next experiment¶

    Ranked for this case:

    1. Solar-frame / present-day phase-space conversion. This is the only tested change here that directly shifts the inferred present-day Galactocentric velocity by the reported amount. The packet’s V⊙,y control changes that velocity and nearly reproduces the I/I total score (packet:2021–2037). The frame definitions differ in V⊙,y, R₀, and z⊙ (frames.py:16–18). The V⊙,y control is therefore strong evidence that the orbit response matters, but it does not establish that the adopted V⊙ value is physically preferable.

    2. Inner force profile and bar forcing. These can change the integrated path, especially for an orbit passing through the bar region, but they cannot change the cluster’s present-day Galactocentric velocity while its heliocentric observables and frame are fixed. The packet reports the profile-shape control as worse at fixed circular speed and describes a pericentre near 1 kpc (packet:2033–2037, 2045–2047). A halo-only normalization test is useful, but \(v_c(R₀)\) alone is a weak label for the force along a 1–7 kpc orbit.

    3. Distance. Distance changes the tangential velocity inferred from proper motion, but the stated 5.43 ± 0.05 kpc prior is too narrow to plausibly supply a 15–20 km/s change by itself. The current W comparison instead uses 5.6 kpc (packet:2014–2015, 2033–2037); the code passes that distance explicitly into the IC conversion (grid4_common.py:40, 48). Recheck the preferred frame at 5.38, 5.43, and 5.48 kpc.

    4. LMC-induced reflex or acceleration. Published reflex signals reach tens of km/s in the distant halo, but are strongest for distant tracers with long dynamical times; they do not imply a 15–20 km/s differential correction for an object confined to the inner few kpc. Treat this as a later model extension, not the first explanation (Petersen & Peñarrubia 2020; Chandra et al. 2024).

    The proposed keep_vc=False sweep is not currently implemented by that option. In axisymmetric_variant, keep_vc=False leaves the halo multiplier at c = 1; only keep_vc=True computes a normalization (mw_variants.py:37–47). Add an explicit halo normalization or target-\(v_c\) parameter. For each target, normalize the axisymmetric potential using the baryon and unit-halo force contributions; record the resulting \(v_c\) and enclosed mass at radii relevant to the orbit.

    I would run the halo-only diagnostic at the existing baseline and at 220, 225, 235, and 240 km/s, with three independent seeds per point: 15 sprays, about 12.5 minutes at 50 seconds each. Keep the Baumgardt IC/projection, distance, PMs, and W bar parameters fixed. Use 215 and 245 only as stress tests if the first sweep has a trend. The packet’s ±36 lnL noise and its single seed make a one-run difference inadequate; require the trend to recur across seeds and exceed that scale before refining. Hold Ωb fixed for this first isolation; if the potential scan produces a credible shift, rescan Ωb around the best point using the same grid for baseline and modified potentials (packet:2039–2047).

    Also run a whole-potential normalization as a separate controlled diagnostic if the aim is specifically to test the packet’s energy-scaling estimate. That preserves the radial shape and bar-to-axisymmetric force ratio while changing the absolute mass scale. The halo-only sweep instead keeps the baryonic bar term fixed in absolute strength and changes its fraction of the total force. The code defines bar amplitude as a multiplier on the difference between barred and axisymmetrized baryonic potentials, added to the selected axisymmetric potential (restricted.py:71–84). Report the bar-force fraction for each halo model; don’t silently interpret fixed bar_amp as fixed relative bar strength. The whole-potential scaling is not exposed by the current make_spray/host_potential interfaces (grid4_common.py:40–47; restricted.py:40–55).

    Literature bounds¶

    A reasonable first-pass local range is roughly 225–235 km/s, with wider points used as diagnostics rather than plausible preferred models. Eilers et al. report \(v_c(R_0)=229.0\) km/s, with 2–5% systematic uncertainty, and exclude the inner 5 kpc because of bar-driven non-axisymmetry (arXiv:1810.09466). McMillan’s mass models give \(232.8\pm3.0\) km/s under the main assumptions, while a weakened \(R_0\) prior gives \(226.8\pm4.2\) km/s (arXiv:1608.00971). Thus 220 is a useful stress/comparison point, but these results do not support treating 215–245 as an unconstrained plausible interval.

    For the inner Galaxy, Portail et al. find \(1.84\pm0.07\times10^{10}M_\odot\) in a specified bulge/bar volume of approximately \(2.2\times1.4\times1.2\) kpc, with model-dependent dark-matter fractions; their inferred bar pattern speed is \(39\pm3.5\) km/s/kpc (arXiv:1608.07954). This constrains the central mass budget, but it is not a spherical enclosed-mass constraint at every point on ω Cen’s orbit. The uncertainties and geometry matter.

    Code audit¶

    • Frame split: make_spray correctly uses separate IC and projection frames; proj_frame or frame means an omitted projection frame falls back to the IC frame (grid4_common.py:40–51). The frame round-trip tests exist, but check PMs, radial velocity, and distance—not sky position or crossed-frame behavior (test_frames_roundtrip.py:6–11).
    • frame_obs_split.py: it reads and executes d1_segments.py up to the literal "\nS = {}" marker (frame_obs_split.py:8–9). This currently avoids the later top-level model loop and file writes (d1_segments.py:45–69), but it is brittle: moving or removing the marker could execute the expensive script. Put single_scores in an import-safe shared module.
    • keep_vc=False: it produces the default halo normalization, not a requested sweep point; the existing test checks default-potential reproduction, not variable halo normalization (mw_variants.py:37–47; test_mw_variants.py:10–14).

    Verified, assumed, and counterargument¶

    Verified by reading code: the IC and projection frames are distinct inputs; the IC conversion uses the selected frame; the sky observables are generated from particle positions; keep_vc=False fixes the halo multiplier to 1; and the frame-split script executes source text up to a marker. The likelihoods, physical velocities, noise estimate, and orbit extrema above are reported in the packet, not independently recomputed (packet:2014–2049).

    Assumptions: the reported scores use comparable inputs and scoring settings; the ±36 figure reasonably represents seed noise; and the literature mass-model constraints are applicable as broad priors to this Hunter+2024 host.

    Strongest argument against my assessment: the V⊙,y-only run nearly reproduces I/I’s total score, and its knee dφ₂ gain cannot come from directly projecting fixed particle positions. That makes an IC/orbit explanation compelling. But the factorial’s large interaction shows the total score still depends strongly on how that altered orbit is projected and scored.

    To be sure: verify the packet scores from the saved sprays, add repeated seeds for the potential sweep, compute \(v_c\) and enclosed mass along the orbit for each model, and test the Ωb response at any promising mass normalization.
