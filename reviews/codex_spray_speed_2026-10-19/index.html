    Codex review: speeding up the AGAMA spray (2026-10-19)¶

    Codex CLI, effort high, read-only; full source of spray.py, restricted.py, mw_variants.py and make_spray supplied.

    The rotating-frame premise is partly wrong: the local AGAMA implementation of Cartesian Omega=... still integrates in inertial coordinates and rotates the potential during force evaluation. Do not expect a major gain from changing frames alone. Combining potential components is a separate optimization. AGAMA orbit.cpp:183, upstream implementation.

    My recommendation: validate accuracy=1e-6 first, benchmark dprkn8 second, then investigate combining the host components. Defer satellite truncation and particle reduction.

    1. Particle accuracy 1e-6: highest-confidence speed opportunity, approximately 1.9×. That is 108/57 from the supplied measurements, not an independently reproduced benchmark. The parameter reaches particle integration while progenitor integration remains at 1e-12, which makes this a relatively isolated experiment. [packet:2018], spray.py:121, spray.py:156.

    Risk/test: numerical errors can systematically favour particular models despite remaining below seed scatter in one example. Compare identical seeds and release epochs at both tolerances across central, boundary, strong-bar and flattened-halo models. Measure paired score changes, changes in model-to-model score differences, and observable distributions. This requires statistical equivalence, not bit identity.

    1. Benchmark method="dprkn8": plausible additional 1.2–1.3×, unmeasured here. AGAMA documents that approximate improvement; the spray currently leaves method unspecified. Its lower-order dense output makes trajectory-derived quantities and final interpolated velocities explicit validation targets. AGAMA reference.tex:1154, AGAMA reference.tex:2180, spray.py:156.

    Risk/test: benchmark it separately at both tolerances against a tighter dop853 reference, retaining the existing trajectory sampling. Check chi, final observables and scores. Do not assume its gain multiplies independently with the tolerance gain.

    1. Combine host components before attempting a frame rewrite: potentially substantial, but no defensible speed multiplier yet. The production path evaluates an axisymmetric component plus separately scaled positive and negative baryonic components. For constant bar speed and size, a static combined representation wrapped in one rotation can be tested while retaining inertial particle integration and the existing satellite trajectory. Slowing bars and additional LMC/spiral terms need separate treatment. restricted.py:83, restricted.py:96, restricted.py:106, restricted.py:40.

    Risk/test: re-expansion introduces force and Hessian errors; Hessians also determine release radii. Compare potential, force and Hessian over the occupied spatial/time domain, including pericentres, the plane and expansion boundaries; then run matched sprays. Preserve the original release calculation initially to isolate integration error. spray.py:32, spray.py:128.

    If using Omega, rotate both positions and inertial velocity components consistently; do not subtract Ω × r from AGAMA’s velocity convention. Satellite-centre interpolation needs separate care because a centre-position derivative in rotating coordinates differs from those velocity components. AGAMA orbit.cpp:104, AGAMA reference.tex:510.

    1. Thread/process tuning: expect limited throughput improvement if eight workers already occupy the available compute capacity. AGAMA parallelizes across orbits; this redistributes the same work rather than reducing force evaluations. AGAMA interface_python.cpp:7871.

    Risk/test: compare eight identical sprays using worker/thread combinations (8,1), (4,2), (2,4), (1,8). Measure completed sprays/hour, peak memory and output identity. Single-spray latency is insufficient. Hardware saturation and scaling remain assumptions.

    1. Cheaper satellite treatment: investigate only after a matched ablation measures its cost. Timing inside agama.orbit does not distinguish host force, satellite force, centre interpolation and integration overhead: the particle call receives their combined potential. spray.py:144, spray.py:156.

    If satellite evaluation occupies fraction f of runtime, eliminating that cost has an optimistic ceiling of 1/(1−f). Risk/test: a distance threshold alone does not establish negligible cumulative deflection or prevent later encounters. Compare several switch radii, retain/re-enable gravity for returning particles, and check scores, stream length, widths and tails. The current calculation retains satellite gravity throughout. spray.py:144.

    1. Fewer particles: approximately proportional runtime savings, with a direct statistical cost. Under ordinary Monte Carlo scaling, halving particles increases scatter by roughly √2; bias correction does not remove that variance. The existing jackknife is a bias correction, not a replacement for seed replication. score_bias.py:75.

    Risk/test: establish both bias and variance using independent replicates at 128k, 256k and a larger reference. Preserve the score definition: the wrapper defaults to pooled scoring, while training records also use fixed and legacy chi bins. grid4_common.py:95, training_set.py:189. Use fewer particles for screening only until those comparisons justify final inference.

    Other proposed changes have little demonstrated upside:

    • Lower trajsize is not result-equivalent. chi is a trapezoidal integral over trajectory samples and controls particle selection/binning. Two samples can preserve endpoints while changing the score. spray.py:160, score.py:299.
    • Python vectorization, release-radius tuning and construction-only caching: approximately 1% maximum benefit in the supplied 108-second benchmark, assuming its timing attribution is complete. Those operations sit outside the measured 107 seconds in orbit integration. [packet:2018].
    • dtype/der: the local AGAMA default already stores trajectories as float32; der=True requests additional deviation-vector calculations. The spray supplies neither option. Casting saved arrays to float64 does not recover discarded precision. AGAMA interface_python.cpp:7524, spray.py:156, training_set.py:186.

    1. Verified versus assumed

    Verified by reading: parameter propagation, combined host/satellite integration, trajectory-dependent chi, AGAMA’s local frame/storage conventions, and existing regression assertions. Subset and chunk tests demand exact array equality; stored-reference tests can skip when their files are absent. tests/test_spray_subset.py:35, tests/test_spray_subset.py:66, tests/test_spray_subset.py:86.

    Assumed: the supplied timings and 30–45 score scatter are representative; production uses the inspected AGAMA implementation; eight workers substantially occupy the machine. I did not reproduce benchmarks or run the regression suite.

    2. Strongest argument against my answer

    Relaxed tolerance could introduce parameter-dependent bias that survives seed averaging and shifts inferred parameters. A more expensive implementation effort that preserves forces could therefore be preferable even with a smaller speed gain. The release calculation’s dependence on host Hessians makes careless re-expansion particularly vulnerable too. spray.py:32.

    3. What I would check to be sure

    • Freeze initial conditions, seed, release epochs, score settings and production AGAMA build for paired comparisons.
    • For purported exact changes, require exact retained arrays and scores. Preserve RNG draw order: initial conditions are generated before subsetting. spray.py:134.
    • For approximate changes, predeclare a systematic score-error budget substantially below 30–45—for example 5—and bound the paired mean error with uncertainty. Also inspect per-star errors, model rankings, track-bin shifts, widths and tails.
    • Establish convergence against tighter integration and denser trajectory sampling; agreement with the current baseline alone does not establish accuracy.
    • Keep optimized outputs separate from reference results, with parameter-complete cache keys and source/input hashes. Existing training resumption explicitly rejects incompatible manifests. training_set.py:155, training_set.py:239.
