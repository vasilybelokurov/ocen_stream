    The premise about Ibata+2019 is already corrected in the project record: its “knee” is a sky feature near the cluster, and the record says no published model reproduces the DR3 proper motion knee at issue here. docs/STREAM_KNEE_INVESTIGATION.md:18–21

    1. What has been tested¶

    “Ruled out” is too strong for several entries. Some tests reject a specific implementation or parameter range; they do not eliminate the broader physical explanation.

    Hypothesis What the record says; how decisive it is
    Code, coordinate, or sign errors Bar timing, force derivatives, centre orbit, frame round trips, arm labels, data integrity, and rotation sense were audited. The recorded checks passed, including round-trip transforms and bar-orientation checks. This is good evidence against those specific implementation errors, not a proof that every pipeline choice is correct. packet:2762–2775 docs/STREAM_DIAGNOSTIC_PLAN.md:14–20
    The measured PM knee is a STREAMFINDER selection artefact An independent Gaia selection found the PM overdensity through phi1≈24, but no independent detection beyond 26. It weakens the selection explanation over the covered range; the steepest part remains unverified independently. packet:2786–2794
    The statistic creates the angle preference or knee misfit Injection tests recovered 28° truth in 100/100 mocks and 20° truth in 20/24. That supports angle recovery in this restricted setup; it does not validate the full likelihood under real model misspecification. The record itself says likelihood differences are overconfident, mock calibration is incomplete, and sensitivity to chi-bin choices remains a check. docs/STREAM_DIAGNOSTIC_PLAN.md:21, 55–60, 104–107 packet:2807–2812 packet:3008–3012
    Cluster rotation Fitted, maximal, reversed, and even 10× rotation runs did not fix the PM mismatch; the measured rotation in the escape region is small. This makes rotation an unlikely fix within those prescriptions, but does not cover every rotating progenitor structure or early history. docs/STREAM_KNEE_INVESTIGATION.md:61–70 packet:2754–2756
    Spray release prescription A tracer comparison was reported to miss the knee at least as badly as the spray, arguing against a spray-only defect. The record notes the 28° tracer comparison was not run, so the test is incomplete. packet:2766–2767 docs/STREAM_DIAGNOSTIC_PLAN.md:61–62
    Debris age or release history Age-cut rescoring at 500, 700, and 1000 Myr left the angle ranking similar; pericentre-concentrated release worsened the fit. That does not test debris older than the available integration or release window. Thus “older debris ruled out” is broader than the evidence. packet:2776–2778 packet:2750–2753
    Axisymmetric host, host mass, or halo shape DB98 and McMillan17 runs did not produce the knee; tested flattening, disc/halo changes at fixed circular speed, and host-wide mass scalings did not solve the joint fit. The mass sweep tested a finite set around Hunter+2024, not arbitrary force profiles. Reject those sampled variants, not potential-shape explanations in general. docs/STREAM_KNEE_INVESTIGATION.md:9–17 packet:2980–3003
    Bar causes the knee A rotating Hunter+2024 bar steepened pmdec and bent the sky track partway; it did not reproduce the full bend. Bar speed, amplitude, size, and angle were then varied. The residual pmdec mismatch persists, and the angle preference is not established: one profile-stage search found the 16°/28° difference within seed noise, while earlier fixed-grid comparisons favored 16°. packet:2374–2399 packet:3014–3024
    Bar model and time dependence Portail+2017, longer-bar variants, and slowing-bar variants were tested. The slowing-bar grid re-scanned today’s pattern speed over 31–34, but only for the listed eta values and with a limited parameter combination; it does not exhaust time-dependent bar histories or bar-phase uncertainty. docs/STREAM_DIAGNOSTIC_PLAN.md:61–62 bin/streams/phase2b_grid.py:2–6, 18–21
    Distance step explains the PM bend The earlier CMD analysis found no ~30% step, but its normalization was later judged unreliable and distance was dropped from the fit. Treat a large step as unsupported by that analysis, not definitively excluded; the distance-free likelihood leaves distance and transverse velocity partly degenerate. docs/STREAM_DIAGNOSTIC_PLAN.md:66–68, 76–83 packet:2893–2897
    Solar frame, omega Cen state, or distance The Ibata frame strongly improved the knee score, but its solar motion conflicts with the stated Sgr A* constraint. Frame decomposition attributed the gain mainly to the cluster’s Galactocentric initial velocity, at a cost near the cluster. PM shifts improved the score toward the edge of the tested ±2σ grid; the profile stage later allowed ±3σ but was sparse. These are important degeneracies, not settled physical fixes. packet:2913–2922 packet:2948–2966 packet:3014–3024
    Omega Cen mass or dark matter Changing cluster mass by factors 0.7 and 1.5 did not move the PM medians into agreement; the larger mass worsened the score. This tests those mass scalings, not self-gravity of old debris. packet:3026–3031
    Sagittarius perturbation The recorded integrations put Sgr at least about 10 kpc from the stream during the tested 600 Myr and estimate a far-too-small differential kick. Strong evidence against Sgr as the recent cause under those assumptions. packet:2350–2357
    LMC and reflex motion Not implemented in the stream model, so untested. The early literature estimate that it was secondary is not a simulation result. docs/STREAM_DIAGNOSTIC_PLAN.md:48–49
    Different progenitor, shared debris, or knee not from recent omega Cen debris Chemistry is suggestive but based on small bright-star samples; the record calls broader XP results inconclusive. Stream 55’s relation and the knee’s ancestry remain open. docs/STREAM_KNEE_INVESTIGATION.md:28–31 packet:2319–2333
    Self-gravity of old debris The cited Ibata+2019 model used live N-body and says earlier debris gravity mattered, but the present project deliberately excludes live N-body. This explanation is outside the allowed model, not ruled out by these tests. docs/STREAM_KNEE_INVESTIGATION.md:121–124, 134–138

    Tests repeated or overlapping: DB98 was used both for debris morphology and for the observed-star backtrack; those address different questions, so the second is not a redundant rerun. Rotation was probed through several related setups (fitted, maximal, and exaggerated), making the repeated negative result more useful but still limited to those implementations. Bar speed and bar size were scanned in successive grids, with some overlap. The larger issue is not literal repetition: “older debris ruled out,” “selection ruled out,” and “the angle is preferred” each overstate what the recorded tests establish. docs/STREAM_KNEE_INVESTIGATION.md:98–102 packet:2374–2399 packet:3014–3024

    2. Is the explanation space exhausted?¶

    No. A useful first gate is still missing: the existing backtrack compares observed stars with omega Cen’s orbit in static McMillan17 and DB98 hosts, over distances 2.5–6.5 kpc and 1.5 Gyr. Its scalar metric is a fixed weighted distance in position and velocity; it does not propagate measurement or cluster-state uncertainty. bin/streams/backtrack_fimbulthul.py:15–16, 34–47

    The rotating-bar machinery exists, including bar speed, present angle, slowing rate, amplitude, and size. That makes the proposed barred backtrack feasible, but the current star-backtrack script does not use it. src/ocen_dm/streams/restricted.py:40–58, 60–89

    Still genuinely open are:

    • Whether knee stars can reach a recent release region in a plausible barred potential once astrometric, RV, distance, and omega Cen state uncertainties are propagated. The current backtrack uses point estimates for the listed observables and a distance grid, and fixes the cluster phase-space point. bin/streams/backtrack_fimbulthul.py:26–47
    • Debris older than the tested window, alternative progenitor structure or ancestry, and live self-gravity (the last is excluded by the PI’s constraint). docs/STREAM_DIAGNOSTIC_PLAN.md:47–49 docs/STREAM_KNEE_INVESTIGATION.md:121–124, 134–138
    • LMC reflex, broader time-dependent bar histories, and potential families beyond the sampled variants. docs/STREAM_DIAGNOSTIC_PLAN.md:45–49
    • The interpretation of the PM ridge beyond phi1≈24, where the independent selection did not recover a clear detection. packet:2789–2794

    3. Is the barred-backtrack “kill test” right?¶

    Yes, as a gate before further tuning, but it needs a better target than “closest to the cluster’s orbit.” The existing test compares star and cluster trajectories using \(dx/0.2\) kpc and \(dv/20\) km/s, then takes the minimum over time and scanned distance. That is a useful diagnostic, but the scales are chosen constants, not propagated uncertainties or a release model. bin/streams/backtrack_fimbulthul.py:2–5, 43–47

    For the bar run, sample each star’s position, PMs, parallax or distance constraint, and RV uncertainty; sample omega Cen’s present state and the allowed host/bar parameters with their relevant constraints. Integrate both trajectories in the same time-dependent potential, with the present bar phase tied to its allowed angle range. Then measure whether the star’s uncertainty region intersects the modeled escape/release distribution—including finite tidal radius and escape velocity—not whether the star and cluster must occupy identical phase space. The bar implementation supports time-dependent rotation and slowing histories. src/ocen_dm/streams/restricted.py:40–58, 60–89

    I would abandon recent omega Cen debris in the tested barred, test-particle framework if the knee-star posterior repeatedly misses every plausible recent release distribution over the model’s relevant release ages, while the same method recovers the near-cluster arm and injected mock debris. That would reject the framework’s recent-release explanation; it would not prove that the stars never came from omega Cen, since older debris, self-gravity, or a different history remain outside that test.

    4. Ranked plan¶

    1. Run the barred reachability test first. Predefine the allowed bar and cluster-state ranges, release-age range, and overlap statistic; calibrate the threshold on near-cluster members and mock releases. Stop tuning if the knee stars remain incompatible across those ranges and mocks pass. Continue if a substantial knee subset overlaps plausible release regions.

    2. Resolve the observational edge and ancestry only if step 1 passes or is ambiguous. Extend an independent Gaia/RV/chemistry check through the distal knee; the current independent PM detection stops short of its steepest part. Go if the same coherent PM track and compatible velocities persist; otherwise describe the distal feature’s membership as unresolved. packet:2789–2794

    3. Only then profile the allowed barred model jointly, with multiple seeds and mocks, including the remaining plausible host, bar-history, and omega Cen state variations. Stop if no fit reproduces the knee within the stated angle/state constraints; write up a negative result: the tested recent test-particle framework can make a partial bend but cannot match the observed PM track. The record already reports the persistent pmdec residual and cautions that the fit ranking is noisy or overconfident. docs/STREAM_DIAGNOSTIC_PLAN.md:10–21, 63–69

    What I verified, what I assume, and the counterargument¶

    Verified: The static backtrack’s time window, distance scan, hosts, and metric are explicit in code. bin/streams/backtrack_fimbulthul.py:15–16, 34–47 The dated record reports the knee stars’ static-host velocity misses and documents the bar, age, selection, host-mass, and frame experiments cited above. docs/STREAM_KNEE_INVESTIGATION.md:98–102 docs/STREAM_DIAGNOSTIC_PLAN.md:55–69 packet:2780–2806

    Assumptions: I take the recorded numerical results and run summaries as accurate; I did not recompute the integrations or inspect every run artifact. “Plausible barred host” needs explicit parameter bounds agreed with the project’s priors.

    Strongest argument against my recommendation: A successful orbit-connection test is not required to be a close pointwise match to the cluster orbit: real escapers leave with offsets, and the record itself notes that Ibata’s explanation invokes self-gravity of earlier debris. Conversely, a failed test can falsely reject ancestry if the distance/RV posterior, old release ages, or allowed bar history is too restrictive. The proposed test is therefore decisive only for the specified recent test-particle model, not for omega Cen ancestry in general. docs/STREAM_KNEE_INVESTIGATION.md:121–124

    To be sure: I would check the exact input uncertainties and membership of the 15 knee stars with RVs, define the bar-angle/pattern-speed prior and release distribution before seeing results, verify the time-dependent orbit integrator against mock releases, and extend the independent PM/RV check beyond phi1≈24. bin/streams/backtrack_fimbulthul.py:28–40 packet:2789–2794
